Why High Accuracy Is Deceptive in Emergency Speech Detection

In natural speech databases for disabled individuals, emergency requests constitute only ~5% of all spoken utterances, with ~95% representing routine general speech.

A naive or uncalibrated classifier that predicts "GENERAL_SPEECH" on every single audio sample achieves 95% overall accuracy while possessing 0% Emergency Recall—leaving every emergency unassisted.

Conversely, lowering the threshold to catch more emergencies triggers frequent false emergency alerts. The False Emergency Rate (FER) measures false alarms as a fraction of routine speech. In Phase 8.1, enforcing FER ≤ 15.0% forced emergency recall down to only 10–20%, mathematically demonstrating the safety infeasibility of unconfirmed clinical deployment.
